## Questions & Answers

**Q1: How are you involved in equipment sharing?**

A: P1 lends equipment to club members. When officers need equipment themselves, they usually approach the lab or faculty advisor rather than borrowing from members. The clubs share the faculty advisor’s lab.

**Q2: What recent lending experience did you describe?**

A: A member with a broken laptop asked P1 for equipment. They needed a laptop for AI agents and a RAG project and wanted to build a small cyberdeck or Tamagotchi-style device. P1 lent his own ESP board and arranged a laptop through the faculty advisor.

**Q3: What was agreed about returning the equipment?**

A: The equipment would be returned when the project was finished. The member might be permitted to keep the ESP-based project, depending on the project and permission. The laptop must be returned.

**Q4: Was there a firm return date?**

A: No. Returning equipment within a week is encouraged, but this arrangement had no hard deadline.

**Q5: How was the loan recorded?**

A: P1 remembered the arrangement. Whether he wrote down this particular loan was uncertain in the interviewer’s recollection.

**Q6: How did you keep track of the borrower?**

A: P1 and the member meet about two or three times a week through the club and shared lab. He treated the loan as ongoing because the equipment had not yet been returned; a separate project-status update was not recalled.

**Q7: Had the equipment been returned?**

A: The current loan was still ongoing. The same member had previously borrowed a laptop and returned it.

**Q8: What has worked well with lending?**

A: P1 described lending as routine: members generally borrow what they need and return it. He did not identify a specific success in the current loan, and no breakage incidents were reported in the discussion.

**Q9: What difficulties have come up?**

A: P1 described occasional uncertainty about equipment status, incomplete notes, and small missing parts. He recalled an actual missing screw bit without a written incident record. Such losses were described as an inconvenience. The shared discussion also raised uncertainty about appropriate use and care; this was not established as misconduct by the current borrower.

**Q10: What did the group say about different equipment and shared access?**

A: The group said that some small club-purchased components may be kept with permission, while more expensive items must return. Taking equipment off campus depends on the relevant supervisor or advisor. They also described occasions when officers could not locate a laptop another officer had borrowed or lent. These were shared accounts, separate from P1’s specific loan.

**Q11: Would you take part in later feedback?**

A: P1 agreed to later feedback on a sketch or prototype.
 
## Synthesis

- **One surprising quote from the shared discussion — P2:** “Not every officer knows which officer lent what to the other members.”
- **One thing I didn’t expect:** Seeing the borrower regularly does not necessarily provide a clear update on whether the equipment is still needed. P1’s current loan had no agreed return date.
- **What this makes me think about the problem:** Equipment owners may need a clearer understanding of current use and return expectations while allowing flexible project timelines. Items that can be kept with permission need to be distinguished from items that remain on loan.
