# Research Synthesis

## Findings

Seven participants described borrowing and lending through personal conversations, office visits, Discord, memory, and simple written notes. P1–P3 were interviewed together, so their shared accounts are not treated as three separate incidents. Six participants were outside the interviewer’s social circle.

Several arrangements worked. P3 returned a laptop without a fixed deadline. P4’s laptop came back that afternoon, and P7 returned equipment within the agreed period. P5 valued camera access for projects and learning.

Difficulties appeared around access and coordination. P2 described officers not knowing what others had lent. P6 described tracking small items as inconvenient, with Discord requests leaving a partial record but no handover or return confirmations. In one delayed request, P5 waited 10–15 minutes, returned about an hour later, and still had to wait until the next day to receive a camera. She described the process as tedious and felt relaxed once she could work. Her separate same-day loan did not involve that delay.

P5 wanted a clearer request channel. P7 independently suggested requesting equipment before visiting. P4 responded positively after the interviewer proposed centralization and raised the challenge of encouraging adoption. These are suggestions and reactions, not evidence that participants have tested a new system.

## Underlying Problem and Surprises

The issue is not simply whether equipment returns. Loan information can stay with particular people, while access depends on reaching someone who can arrange the loan.

A surprising finding was that flexible return arrangements could succeed. Trust reduced worry without necessarily making equipment status clear to everyone involved. A proposed change should preserve the convenience participants value.

## Problem Statement

Students and campus equipment lenders sometimes have difficulty coordinating access and establishing who currently holds an item because requests and loan information depend on individual availability, memory, and scattered records. This can lead to searching, waiting, or additional follow-up, even when equipment is eventually returned.

## How Might We

How might we help students and campus equipment lenders coordinate access and understand current loan status while preserving the ease and flexibility of informal borrowing?

## Supporting Evidence

- **P2:** Identified gaps in officers’ knowledge of equipment loans.
- **P5:** Described a specific request delayed until the next day after waiting and revisiting the lab.
- **P6:** Described tracking inconvenience and requests without completion confirmations.
- **P7:** Independently suggested requesting equipment before visiting.
